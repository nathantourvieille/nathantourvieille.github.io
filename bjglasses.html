Blackjack Glasses

Developing a wearable, robust computer vision system capable of identifying and tracking playing cards under heavy occlusion and varied lighting, ultimately providing real-time ideal Blackjack strategy based on highest expedted value.

Project Introduction

Blackjack is unique in the casino world because it is the only game that can be mathematically beaten over the long run. Professional players achieve this through card counting, assigning a score to every card to maintain a "running count." In a standard Hi-Lo system, Aces through 10s are valued at -1, 2s through 6s at +1, and the rest at 0.

My goal for this project was to go beyond simple counting by building a system that keeps track of every single card played. With this complete data, I can construct an algorithm to calculate the highest possible expected value (EV) for any given hand.

The foundation of this project is a custom computer vision system. For the strategy to work, the system must identify the suit and rank of all visible cards and track them across frames to ensure no card is overcounted. This CV pipeline is built on three core components:

Card Extraction and Normalization Pipeline

This initial pipeline isolates individual cards from a raw dataset to create standardized clean card crops. These card crops will then be used to generate scenes with multiple cards for training.

Segmentation via Otsu’s Thresholding

To isolate the card from a dark background, I implemented Otsu’s Binarization. This method provided superior robustness compared to basic contour detection by automatically calculating the optimal pixel intensity threshold to separate the subject from background noise. The resulting boundary is significantly smoother than a standard Canny contour processed via approxPolyDP.

Geometric Line-Fitting

Rather than relying on noisy or rounded contour corners, the pipeline samples points along the four edges of the card. It fits a mathematical line to each side using Linear Regression (L2 Distance), ensuring the edges are perfectly straight and mathematically defined.

Virtual Corner Intersection

The true corners are identified by calculating the intersections of these four lines. This allows the system to determine where the corners would be if the physical card didn’t have rounded edges.

Perspective Correction

Using the four calculated corners, I applied a Homography Matrix to rectify the image. This transforms the tilted perspective of the original photo into a flat, top-down view with a standardized aspect ratio.

Alpha Channel Masking and Cleanup

Finally, a synthetic card-shaped mask is generated and overlaid. By eroding the mask by a few pixels, the process crops out residual background and edge artifacts. This results in a clean digital asset ready for scene composition.

Synthetic Card Dataset Generation Pipeline

This pipeline creates complex scenes composed of multiple cards. As I just wanted to make a basic prototype, I only made scenes with Aces and Kings, as well as the back side of the card from three different decks. It is designed to produce reasonable training data with accurate segmentation labels under heavy occlusion, perspective distortion, and challenging lighting conditions.

1. Geometric Transformations

Cards first undergo randomized geometric augmentation to simulate real-world viewpoints:

  • Scaling
  • Rotation
  • Perspective warping

All geometric operations are applied identically to both the RGB image and its mask to guarantee exact alignment.

2. Surface and Sensor Augmentations

To improve model generalization, a series of appearance-based augmentations are applied: Specular glare and lighting gradients, motion and Gaussian blur, brightness and contrast variation, HSV color jitter, and sensor noise. These effects simulate camera artifacts while preserving the integrity of the underlying segmentation mask.

3. Scene Composition with Explicit Occlusion Handling

Cards are placed onto high-resolution random backgrounds. Since Blackjack involves frequent overlaps, half of the generated scenes feature high occlusion fanned configurations. Each card is assigned a depth, and occlusions are handled explicitly:

  • For each card, a visible mask is computed by subtracting the masks of cards layered above it.
  • Only the visible pixels of each card are blended into the final scene.
  • Feathered alpha blending is applied to produce realistic edges.

4. Segmentation and Bounding Box Extraction

After compositing the scene, visible masks are converted into simplified polygon contours for compact representation. The pipeline then generates a standardized text file containing all classes and corresponding polygons present in the scene.

Inference Strategy: Solving the Multi-Deck Ambiguity

While all similar projects I found online detect corner pips, this method would likely not be robust enough for a real casino environment. My objective was to build a system that would handle edge cases as the room for error in Blackjack card counting is close to none.

The Pip Problem and Multi-Deck Collision

Identifying cards solely by their pips introduces two potentially significant logic failures:

  • Occlusion Vulnerability: If pips are covered by another card or a hand, the model loses the card entirely, even if the majority of the card body is visible.
  • Identity Ambiguity: In a standard six-deck shoe, multiple identical cards often overlap. A pip-only detector seeing two identical pips in close proximity cannot reliably determine if it is observing one card or two cards stacked.

Architectural Evolution

I pivoted towards identifying and tracking the entire card geometry through several iterations:

  • Iteration 1 (YOLO Bounding Boxes): Initial tests used standard bounding boxes. However, in high occlusion arrangements, a rectangular box for one card could often encompass its neighbor, causing ground truth noise.
  • Iteration 2 (Instance Segmentation): Transitioned to Instance Segmentation. This allowed the polygon label to represent only the visible part of the card. The model learned to distinguish the subtle contrast between overlapping borders.
  • Iteration 3 (Two-Stage Classification): Experimented with a modular approach: using a YOLO segmentation model trained on a "card" class to generate region proposals, passing those crops into a custom ResNet-based CNN in PyTorch.

Final Implementation: The One-Shot Approach

The one-shot instance segmentation approach proved much more effective. Using a test set of Aces and Kings, the model achieved: Real-Time Inference on MacBook hardware, High Precision under heavy occlusion/blur, and Tracking IDs based on temporal presence.

Next Steps

The next phase involves expanding to the full 52-card deck. I will monitor for classification confusion between similar ranks like 6s and 9s. If resolution limits hinder the one-shot approach, I will revisit the two-stage CNN architecture. I am also exploring pose detection to infer corner locations under total occlusion and doing a deep dive into improving tracking robustness to avoid catastrophic ID switches.
